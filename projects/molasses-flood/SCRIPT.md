# The Sweetest Disaster: the Great Molasses Flood (Boston, 15 Jan 1919)

## Facts (with sources)
- Tank ~50 ft tall, 90 ft wide, held ~2.3 million gallons of molasses; owned by Purity Distilling / United States Industrial Alcohol, on Commercial Street in the North End. (Britannica; newengland.com)
- Burst ~12:30-12:40 pm, 15 Jan 1919. Rivets popped with a sound witnesses compared to a machine gun. (Britannica; csmonitor)
- Wave ~25 ft high at peak, ~35 mph. Several blocks flooded 2-3 ft deep. (Wikipedia; boston.gov)
- Buckled the Atlantic Ave elevated railway, knocked a streetcar/train off its tracks, pushed a firehouse off its foundation. (boston.gov)
- 21 dead, ~150 injured. (CS Monitor; Time)
- Tank filled with too little water to check for leaks; treasurer Arthur Jell, who oversaw it, had no engineering background. (Wikipedia)
- Leaked so badly it was painted brown to hide the leaks; locals collected leaked molasses for their homes. (Wikipedia; boston.gov)
- Temperature rose from ~2°F to ~41°F in the days before. (Wikipedia)
- 2016 Harvard/APS study (Nicole Sharp): cold air thickened the molasses fast, trapping victims and hampering rescuers; "slow as molasses in January" quote. (APS News; Insurance Journal; Britannica)
- Cleanup with salt water from a fireboat plus sand; harbor brown until summer. (Wikipedia; boston.gov)
- Company blamed anarchists; court-appointed auditor held it responsible, ~$628,000 damages. (Wikipedia)
- Afterward Boston required engineers' signed/sealed plans with calculations. (Time; boston.gov)
- Locals said the area smelled sweet on hot days for decades; tank's base is now under a Little League field in Langone Park. (Wikipedia)

## Script (~60 s)
1. HOOK: In 1919, a wave of molasses as tall as a house chased people through Boston at thirty-five miles an hour.
2. SETTING: Boston's North End. A steel tank fifty feet tall, holding two point three million gallons of the stuff, waiting to become alcohol.
3. ACTION:
   - The man in charge wasn't an engineer. He tested it with barely any water.
   - It leaked so much, neighbours filled buckets at the seams.
   - The company's fix? Paint the tank brown.
   - Then, in mid-January, the city warmed from two degrees to forty-one.
4. SNAP: Just after noon, the rivets popped like gunfire... and the whole tank split open.
5. DEADPAN: Luckily, molasses is famously slow. Unluckily, nobody told this molasses.
   It bent the elevated railway, shoved a firehouse off its foundation, and flooded the streets three feet deep. Twenty-one people died.
6. EXPLANATION: Why so deadly? Take honey from the fridge: it barely moves. Warm it, and it pours. The molasses poured out warm and fast, then the January air thickened it like glue around everyone it caught. Rescuers could barely wade in.
7. CONSEQUENCE: The company blamed anarchists. The court blamed the tank. And from then on, big structures in Boston needed an engineer's signature and the maths to prove it.
8. CALLBACK: The tank's base now sits under a Little League field. And on hot summer days, locals swore the North End still smelled sweet... for decades.
