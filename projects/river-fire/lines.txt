In 1969, a river in Cleveland caught fire. And it wasn't even the first time.

The Cuyahoga River ran straight through the steel mills, and Cleveland was proud of every smokestack.

The mills fed the river oil. Then grease. Then logs, tires and trash, until the water barely moved. It had already burned at least a dozen times.

On a Sunday in June, a train rolled over a wooden bridge. One spark dropped... and the river went up in flames.

Luckily, firefighters put it out in about twenty minutes. Unluckily, that was so fast, nobody took a single photo.

So when Time magazine told the story, it simply used a picture of a much bigger fire. From 1952.

Here's the thing: water can't burn. But oil floats, like the fat on top of cold soup. Pour in enough, and the river wears a blanket of fuel.

Millions saw that photo. Within three years, America had an Environmental Protection Agency, and the Clean Water Act.

Fifty years later, the river made the news again. This time, its fish were safe to eat.
