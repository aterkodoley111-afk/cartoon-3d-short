# The River That Caught Fire (Cleveland, 1969)

## Facts (with sources)
- 22 Jun 1969 (a Sunday), ~noon: oil-slicked debris on the Cuyahoga River in the Cleveland Flats was lit by a spark from a train crossing a railroad trestle. Wooden debris was caught against the pilings of two wooden railroad trestles. (Ideastream/WOSU 2019; Great Lakes Now; Ohio History Central)
- The fire lasted about 20-30 minutes, put out by land crews and a city fireboat; ~$50,000 damage to railroad bridges ($45,000 Norfolk & Western + $5,000 Newburgh & South Shore). (Smithsonian 2019; Cleveland Magazine)
- It was so short that no one photographed it. (Smithsonian; Ideastream)
- The river had burned at least a dozen times before: first recorded 1868; 1883, 1887, 1912 (killed five), 1922, 1936, 1941, 1948, 1952. (Earth Magazine; Great Lakes Now; Smithsonian)
- The 1952 fire was the worst: ~$1.5 million damage. (Cleveland Magazine; Great Lakes Now)
- The Plain Dealer gave the 1969 fire 181 words. (Earth Magazine 2016)
- Time magazine, 1 Aug 1969, used a photo of the 1952 fire; that issue (Apollo 11 + Chappaquiddick) was one of its most-read. (WBUR 2019; Great Lakes Now)
- The fires + Time story are credited as a catalyst for the EPA (Dec 1970) and the Clean Water Act (1972). (WPSU/NPR 2019; ELI)
- Rivers do not burn; the floating oil slick and debris did. (Ideastream "five myths")
- March 2019: Ohio EPA / US EPA lifted the fish-consumption impairment; fish from the river declared safe to eat. (AP/PBS 19 Mar 2019; cleveland.com)

## Analogy
Oil floats on water like fat on cold soup: the water can't burn, the fuel layer on top can.
