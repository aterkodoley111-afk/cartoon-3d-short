Half a mountain fell down in seconds. It fell so hard, it climbed the next mountain.
January ninth, 1965, before dawn. Highway Three, near a town called Hope, British Columbia.
A small snow avalanche had blocked the road below Johnson Peak.
A yellow convertible got stuck in it. An oil tanker stopped behind it. Then a hay truck.
At two minutes to seven, the mountain let go.
Forty-seven million cubic metres of rock came down.
It flattened the forest. It swallowed a whole lake.
It shot across the valley, up the other side... then slid back down, like water sloshing in a bathtub.
Luckily, a Greyhound bus had stopped just short of it.
Sadly, four people were already there. Two of them have never been found.
A seismograph had recorded an earthquake, so officials blamed one.
But scientists later worked out the earthquake was the slide itself.
Johnson Peak had been cracked for thousands of years, like a stale cookie with a split down the middle.
Nothing pushed it. Gravity just finally won.
The highway was rebuilt. Not around the slide. On top of it.
So today, drivers cruise fifty-five metres above the old road... on the mountain that disappeared.
