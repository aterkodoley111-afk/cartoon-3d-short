# The Mountain That Suddenly Disappeared — The 1965 Hope Slide (approved v4)

## Facts (with sources)
- Jan 9, 1965, Nicolum Valley, Hope-Princeton Highway (BC Hwy 3), ~18 km SE of Hope, BC, below Johnson Peak. (Wikipedia "Hope Slide"; CBC 2020)
- ~4 a.m.: a small snow avalanche blocked the highway. A yellow convertible got stuck; an Arrow Transfer oil tanker stopped behind it; a loaded hay truck stopped behind the tanker. (Wikiwand/Wikipedia; CBC; TranBC)
- 6:58 a.m.: about half of Johnson Peak collapsed; ~47 million m³ of rock, mud and debris. (Ashcroft-Cache Creek Journal; TranBC; CBC)
- Debris crossed the valley, swept up Outram Lake, ran up the slope of Mount Coulter opposite, hung there, then flowed back down ("sloshing"). Up to ~85 m deep (some sources up to 500 ft), ~3 km of highway buried. (Ashcroft-Cache Creek Journal; Tourism Hope; Wikipedia)
- Four people died; two (Dennis Arlitt, Mary Kalmakoff) were never found and remain under the debris. (604now; Wikipedia)
- A Greyhound bus heading toward the site stopped/turned back just before the slide. (Wikipedia; TranBC)
- Seismographs (Penticton) recorded "earthquakes" at 3:56 and 6:58; Highways Minister Phil Gaglardi believed an earthquake did it. Later analysis (Evans et al.; Weichert et al. 1994): the seismic signals were the slides themselves; no seismic trigger. (Hope Standard; Wikipedia; Kellar 2005 summary)
- Cause: a mountain riddled with faults/fractures and weak volcanic layers that slowly deformed and gave way to gravity. (Hope Standard; Brideau et al. 2005)
- The new highway and viewpoint are built ON the slide debris, ~55 m above the original ground. (Hope Slide marker, HMDB)

## Script (v4, hook without the year, ~196 words)
Half a mountain fell down in seconds. It fell so hard, it climbed the next mountain.
January ninth, 1965, before dawn. Highway Three, near a town called Hope, British Columbia.
A small snow avalanche had blocked the road below Johnson Peak.
A yellow convertible got stuck in it. An oil tanker stopped behind it. Then a hay truck.
At two minutes to seven, the mountain let go.
Forty-seven million cubic metres of rock came down.
It flattened the forest. It swallowed a whole lake.
It shot across the valley, up the other side... then slid back down, like water sloshing in a bathtub.
Luckily, a Greyhound bus had stopped just short of it.
Sadly, four people were already there. Two of them have never been found.
A seismograph had recorded an earthquake, so officials blamed one.
But scientists later worked out the earthquake was the slide itself.
Johnson Peak had been cracked for thousands of years, like a stale cookie with a split down the middle.
Nothing pushed it. Gravity just finally won.
The highway was rebuilt. Not around the slide. On top of it.
So today, drivers cruise fifty-five metres above the old road... on the mountain that disappeared.
